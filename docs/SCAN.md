# Case scan

Phone page: `/scan`. Portfolio links to it. `POST /api/scan` is the same call for a service token.

A `box_2d` whose four numbers are all 1 or less is already a fraction of the photo. Anything larger is the 0–1000 scale. The edge check runs before rounding, so a box that only crosses the photo after rounding is kept. That read lives in the Worker, so production needs `npx wrangler deploy --config worker/wrangler.toml` after this merges. Do not deploy from a branch. `GET /scan` reports how many scans are left and does not use one.

On a phone with a camera, `/scan` opens the rear camera. Hold the case still for about a second and the phone sends that full-resolution frame to `/api/scan`. Steadiness compares a downscaled greyscale frame with the one the spell started from. A pan counts, measured across the width and the height separately, so a sideways move of about a fifth of a portrait frame is a new view. A small tremble or a brighter exposure does not. Two reads in a row that were taken while the phone was moving show “Hold still, tap to scan” and wait until one frame after another has been steady for three seconds, or until a tap. A tap during the shutter wait is kept and runs when the shutter is ready. The cue stays up until a scan actually starts, and it is hidden while that scan is reading. A tremble under about 6% of the frame keeps the labels where they are when the aligned pictures disagree. When they still agree, the labels move by that small shift. A real slide keeps them, moved by that shift, only when the pictures still agree after aligning. A swapped slab on that slide, or a larger change, drops the read and scans the steady view again instead of staying on “moved, rescanning”. A disagreement on its own, with the shift still under 6%, does not forget the cards already seen. While that photo is in flight the screen says reading case, about 30s. Three scans in a row that add no new card pause with “Paused to save scans, move or tap to resume”. That pause is measured from the frame the reading returned on, not the frame that was sent, so the motion during the read does not undo the pause. If that frame is missing, the old anchor is dropped. A slide of about a fifth of the frame resumes it. So does a row that really changed, judged after dividing each frame by its local brightness, once that residual reaches about 15. Darkness is the brightest half of the frame, not the average, so a shadow on one side does not count. Automatic scans run only when that half is at grey 18 or more and the too-dark cue is off, so a label with no shift is a tap. Below about grey 17.5 for a second the cue turns on, the screen says “Too dark to track, add light or tap to scan”, and a tap is placed without a shift. The message stays up until that half has been at grey 18 or more for a second. A read that was sent while the case could be tracked, and comes back too dark, is dropped. That drop skips the pause only when the cue is already up. A swap there needs a tap. In dim light that can still be tracked, a read whose local-mean pictures disagree is dropped. A dim read places its labels with the local-mean shift, because the raw match reads as no movement. A shadow does not resume it, including a dark half of the frame and a shadow combined with a small sway. A dim frame trusts the shift measured after that division, because the raw match misses the pan. A 15% pan does not resume on its own, and neither does a dim wobble under that, even when the shift reads as zero, unless the local-mean residual reaches about 20. A swap of one to three slabs needs a tap. A tap resumes it too. A read that the phone moved during does not count toward that pause. A read dropped because the light changed, with the phone still under 6%, does count, and three of those pause. An unread slab matches the last one when the boxes still overlap by half after that shift. A row that clears that local-mean gate counts unread slabs as new, and cards that were already read stay remembered. A light that blinks on and off does not restart the three-second hold while the phone is steady. Labels sit on the cards: the code and grade that were read, an EN or JP tag, the street price, and the sticker when it was read. Green means the sticker is more than 10% under street. Amber is 5% to 10% under. A missing sticker stays neutral. A JP card turns green or amber only against a manual JP price on a watch or holding row saved as JP. An unread language shows `?`. A card with no slab is labeled Raw. A stale English street is shown and marked stale. If the phone has moved by the time the reading returns, the colored labels stay off and the view says it is rescanning. A failed read retries once on that still view, then waits until the phone moves. With no JP comp the label says **no JP comp** and is not compared with an English price. The same steady view is not scanned again. Automatic scans wait four seconds apart, and the corner shows how many scans are left today. Pause holds the camera. Freeze keeps the last reading and opens the list with the lot total. **Take photo** and **Upload photo** stay available. Without a camera, or if permission is refused, the page uses the file picker. A desktop pointer starts on Upload photo.

Send one photo: a camera shot, an upload, or an `imageUrl`. The URL is fetched only as an image. The listing page is not opened, and the photo is not stored. **Save scan** keeps the reading on the phone, not the bytes.

The vision Worker finds each card, reads a slab label before the card face, and returns a box, a grid position, a print guess, a language, a sticker, and a condition estimate. The estimate is not a grade and not a PSA prediction. **Close-up check** opens `/pregrade` for a front and back of one card.

A card is priced only when the language was read as English, the print is one exact id, the confidence is at least 0.8, and the stored English quote is fresh. Anything else says **No price yet** and stays out of the lot sum. A Japanese card, including a label that says JP even when the set name says EN, is not given an English price. A sticker more than 10% under a fresh English street price is marked under street. A caption can place prices in grid order, including `1288 (Sequential x4)` and `X` for not for sale.

**Max lot offer** opens `/bid` with the target set to 0.9 times the fresh English street total of the cards that clear. The rate, fees and GST are still typed there. The offer is not a bid.

A caption may put several prices on one line. Slashes or new lines separate rows. `X` is not for sale. `0` is no price. `(Sequential xN)` stays attached to the price in front of it.

A label with no language word falls back to a confident English read from the model. English rules text counts as English. Japanese card text counts as Japanese and is not priced as English. A JP word on the label stays Japanese. A BGS 10 is not priced as a PSA 10. Each unpriced card says why. A promo that is not in the seed says **Promo not in price data**. Japanese comps for a Japanese card are a follow-up, not this page.

A slab number such as `#106` is joined to the set on the same label (`EB04` becomes `EB04-106`) and looked up. The echoed code is that full id. A year is not used as a number.

Liked names (Luffy, Nami, Boa, Sanji manga) come from the print's character. OP14-118 "You'll Frighten Me..." is Boa. Sanji counts only on a manga print. When the print was not found, the flag falls back to the name that was read.

OP09-004p4 is Shanks, 3rd Anniversary Silver, rarity SP. Raw has no stored price. PSA 10 is a manual PriceCharting figure of US$1,350.50 on 2026-10-18, at `shanks-silver-op09-004`. Three eBay sold comps are kept as evidence only: US$961 (2026-10-07), US$1,226.02 (2026-10-02), US$1,425 (2026-09-09). A PSA label `OP13 EN / SHANKS / 3RD ANNIVERSARY-SILVER / #004` resolves to that print. OP13-004 itself stays Sabo. `EN 2ND ANNIVERSARY SET` is OP-09, so Luffy `#061` is `OP09-061`. `PB STORAGE BOX SET-GOLD` is OP-08, so Bonney `#105` is `OP08-105`. If the set name does not map, the name and number are searched across the seed and returned as candidates. On PRB02, OP12, OP14 and OP15, a label that says ALTERNATE ART matches the manga print.

A full code that is not an English print in the seed says **print not in seed**. A label that really says JP still says **JP is not priced as English**. A BGS Black Label is recorded and is not priced; pricing one is a follow-up.

A fresh manual English watch price for the same print and grade counts ahead of the seed, including in the lot total and the max-lot offer. A stale seed figure is shown with its date and stays out of the lot total. Picking a print keeps that watch price. **Max lot offer** opens `/bid` with the target, the house, the ask currency, and each clearing card. The bid page splits the solved hammer across those cards. A USD lot ask is not subtracted from the S$ street.

Boxes are `box_2d` values from 0 to 1000. A box past the photo, or one that disagrees with its row and column, is dropped. The scan returns those boxes and does not build a crop for every card. The phone crops the photo it already has, JPEG quality 0.8, long edge at most 1600px, and never enlarges it. The row shows that native long edge. Under 600px it says **too small for a close-up**. One card can be cropped on the server with `POST /scan/crop` (same 1600px cap, no daily photo cap). An empty 503 from that call is retried once. **Close-up check** opens `/pregrade` with that crop so the front photo is not blank. A scan that fails gives its cap slot back.

A full code the seed does not have, including an EB04 number, stays the code that was read and says **print not in seed**. Card number 000 is not a print. A name guess does not replace that read code.

Street prices in S$ use `usdToSgd` and `data/fx-usd-sgd.json`, the same ECB table as the book. The photo cap uses the Asia/Singapore day. On staging the counter is a Durable Object; without that binding it stays in KV. PRB-02 prints are already in `prb02-en-seed.csv`.
