# D03 — New player / parent

Seat: parent or first-timer who just left Bandai Asia (`https://asia-en.onepiece-cardgame.com/`) and/or Bandai how-to-play. Those sites were instantly the One Piece card game. Piecebook’s Start does not say that. “Play” is our word. I do not know it means *learn the TCG*. Job: first-fold copy and a picture so a stranger taps through to `/learn`.

## First impression of current Start (2 sentences)

`phone-start.png` is a quiet notebook: **Piecebook**, a find-a-card search, then three text doors whose big type is Collect / Play / World. No box, no Luffy, no “card game.” I came from a site that already showed me starter boxes and “ONE PIECE CARD GAME”; here Play could be a match, an app, or the Decks tab, and the only “New to the game?” line sits *under* the doors and still says Play.

Learn itself (`phone-learn.png`, first screenful) is the page I would read to a kid — DON!! and Life labeled, then the turn — but I have not clicked through yet. The miss is the door, not the room.

## Collectr as a place (2 sentences)

Collectr’s first glass is a card shop: Find a Product, Shop, a cart, then One Piece as Cards Only, Price high to low, SAMPLE on the art, a large USD and a % on every tile. I knew in one glance I was in a price tracker, not a how-to-play page — and I would not hand a child that tape.

## Bandai Asia as a place (2 sentences)

Bandai Asia is the official TCG in one screen: the words “ONE PIECE CARD GAME,” anniversary hero, starter and booster faces, events, Quick Beginner’s Guide, Teaching App. I never wondered what site I was on. How-to-play is the same place in a louder funnel (video, READ THE GUIDE, six starter photos, official shop, app) — still the card game, still not five sentences I can read aloud.

## Top 5 homepage changes for this job

1. **High — Name the TCG in the first line under the wordmark.** “Piecebook” alone is a notebook. One body line in ink, no accent: *One Piece cards. How a game works, what they cost, the story so far.* A stranger from Bandai Asia must read “card game” / “how a game works” before they meet Collect.

2. **High — The learn door’s big type cannot be Play.** Visitor title: **How the card game works.** Subline: *Five sentences you can read to a kid, then Bandai’s own rules.* House name Play stays muted meta, or drops. The door is a `Link` to `/learn` (already the route). Do not promise “build a deck from your cards” on this door — that is how Play used to dump a parent into empty Decks.

3. **High — Put ST-21 Gear 5 (fallback ST-08 Luffy) on that door, and get the face onto the first fold.** Allowed art: TCGplayer starter-box CDN, letterbox on white, same as a Sets tile. A kid who watches the show will point at Gear 5. On a 390-wide column, a 3∶2 band on *Collect first* means the starter box never appears above the fold — so the learn door has to lead on phone, or its art has to sit in the first fold while Collect/World wait. No video embed. No official shop tile. No accent fill on the door (paper surface, line, ink type).

4. **Med — Stop using Play in the footnote.** “New to the game? Play has the rules…” still teaches the pillar word. Same job, visitor words, same `/learn` link: *New to the card game? How a game works, in five sentences.* The footnote is a backup, not the door.

5. **Low — Do not let search + Collect own the first glance.** Keep `Find a card or a box` — that is the other stranger’s job — but it is a field, not the hero. If search and a tall Collect box sit above How the card game works, I will think this is a catalog and leave for Bandai’s Teaching App.

## 3 things to keep

- Quiet paper / ink, unofficial line, no cart. I can tell a kid this is not Bandai’s store.
- `/learn` as written: two labeled words, then the five sentences, then 36 starter decks, then Bandai outbound. That is the room the door must open.
- Tab labels and `/belong` stay. I will find Sets and World later. Night one I need the TCG.

## One trade

Bandai’s recognition (a starter-box face a parent already saw) without Bandai’s store (no video embed, no shop, no app, no accent on the door). Piecebook keeps the five sentences you can read aloud; the box is only so a stranger knows *which* game those sentences are for.
